Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers for teachers, musicians, and partners.

What is Cadance?

Cadance is a dance class music app built to keep classes moving with fast access, class‑friendly controls, and reliable playback. It's the sequel to BarreNotes.

Why doesn't Cadance support Spotify or other streaming services?

Cadance needs playback access that supports dance-specific controls such as tempo changes, repeats, countdowns, and reliable class playback. Spotify and most other streaming services do not provide third-party apps the playback rights and technical access needed for those controls. Cadance supports music in your device library, imported local files, Apple Music on supported tiers, and Syllabus material.

What music licensing issues should studios and teachers consider?

Streaming services are intended for personal consumption. An Apple Music subscription and/or a Cadance subscription gives you access to playback tools; it does not itself grant public-performance or studio-use rights for the music.

Those rights are a publisher and rights-holder issue, separate from the tools used to play the music. Requirements vary by territory and use, so studios and teachers should contact the relevant publisher, rights holder, or local licensing body for guidance.

Cadance combines free Class Planning Mode, flexible passes, and optional subscriptions. New users receive 14 free Day Passes and 1 free Perfect Practice Pass. A Day Pass unlocks a full teaching day; a Perfect Practice Pass keeps one selected song ready for 30 days. Day Passes never expire. See current local pricing in the App Store.

What can I do with Cadance for free?

Class Planning Mode includes browsing, playlists, Teacher Notebook, sharing, search and discovery, and 15-second previews of any track. Linked music can be read, shared, and sampled by everyone; sustained playback follows the relevant pass, subscription, and music-source access rules.

What happens when I play a track past 15 seconds?

The 15-second limit appears only when the track is not covered by your current subscription or other active access. For example, it can apply when you have no playback subscription, or when you try to play a DRM-protected track with a Spark subscription. A banner then tells you a Day Pass will activate in 10 seconds. Let it activate to unlock the full teaching day. Once activated, you have unrestricted access until midnight local time — no further prompts, no interruptions during class.

If you cancel activation, Cadance will not ask again during the current session. For the rest of that session, any track not covered by your current access ends at the 15-second preview limit. To activate a pass manually after cancelling, go to Settings > Day Passes > Activate a Day Pass Now.

Do Day Passes expire?

No. Day Passes never expire. They stay in your account until you activate them, ready whenever you teach.

Do Day Passes sync between my iPhone and iPad?

Yes, via iCloud. Sign into the same iCloud account on both devices and your balance, activation status, and purchase history stay in sync automatically.

Can I subscribe AND buy Day Passes?

Yes. A Day Pass temporarily unlocks Patron-tier access regardless of your current subscription. For example, a Spark subscriber can buy a Day Pass to access Apple Music streaming for a one-off workshop without upgrading their subscription.

That won't happen. Once a Day Pass activates, you have unrestricted access until midnight. Cadance will never interrupt a class. The only check happens at the 15-second mark on your first sustained track of the day — well before any class begins.

Definition of cadence

Cadence refers to a rhythmic sequence or flow of sounds in language, music, or activity. It can describe the beat or measure of rhythmical motion, such as in music or military chants.

Musical cadence

In music, a cadence marks the end of a phrase, creating a sense of resolution.

  • Authentic cadence: a strong resolution, typically moving from a dominant chord (V) to the tonic (I).
  • Half cadence: ends on a dominant chord, creating an incomplete feeling.
  • Plagal cadence: moves from the subdominant (IV) to the tonic (I), often called the "Amen cadence."
  • Deceptive cadence: leads you to expect a resolution that does not occur.
Cadence in language

In speech, cadence supports clarity and expression — the rise, fall, and timing that make phrases land.

Military cadence

In marching, cadence refers to rhythmic chants that help maintain pace and unity of movement.

Exercise cadence

Cadence can also refer to the rhythm or tempo at which a physical exercise is performed. In activities like running, cycling, or weightlifting, cadence is often measured in beats per minute (BPM), repetitions per minute (RPM), or steps per minute (SPM). Understanding cadence helps optimize performance, enhance endurance, and reduce injury risk.

Why cadence matters for dance classes

Cadence helps define structure and emotional impact in music, improves flow in language, and supports synchronization in movement. In class, that translates into clean starts, consistent tempo, and less interruption.
